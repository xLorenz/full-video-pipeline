# Stage 7 — Audio & Music (Immersive Sound)

> Sound is the second sense the viewer experiences every cut, transition, and
> animation through. Silent graphics are half as stimulating. Music tells the
> viewer how to feel — an engagement superpower no visual can replace.

## 1. Build the library first

Gather before cutting: movement sounds (whooshes), highlight sounds
(sparkles, clicks, pops), transition sounds (shutters, pops), plus the
emotional trio below and 3–5 music beds matched to the Stage 1 mood arc.
Cutting with sounds at hand keeps every visual decision sonically supported;
hunting sounds mid-edit guarantees silent stretches.

## 2. Sound-design pass (every visual gets a sound)

Walk the timeline and add a sound wherever something moves, appears, or is
emphasized:

- Movement → whoosh (scaled to the motion: small slide, small whoosh).
- Highlight / reveal → sparkle, highlight ping, or pop.
- Entrance that was animated in (Stage 6) → the sound that motivates it.

Why: double stimulation. The viewer feels each moment twice, which deepens
encoding and immersion. Missing sounds are not neutral — they are flat.

## 3. The emotional trio (use with discipline)

- **Risers** build tension and anticipation: something important is about to
  happen. Use ONLY when something important actually happens next. Empty
  risers burn trust and the technique stops working.
- **Hits** release tension and emphasize moments: verdicts, reveals, impacts.
  Solo for punch, or after a riser for full build-and-release. Do not stack
  hits back-to-back — emphasis everywhere is emphasis nowhere.
- **Drones** create mystery, intrigue, and underlying suspense for darker
  moods. Low, continuous, felt more than heard. Reserve for stretches where
  uncertainty should hum underneath.

Why they work: they directly manipulate emotion, and emotional investment is
engagement. A viewer who feels suspense, triumph, or dread keeps watching to
resolve the feeling.

Common mistake: riser-hit-drone carpet-bombing. Three well-placed trios beat
thirty decorative ones.

## 4. Music by mood blocks

1. Split the video by subject change.
2. Assign the desired viewer emotion per block (anticipation, innovation/
   excitement, unease, triumph, reflection).
3. Pick the track that creates that feeling — not the track you like, the
   track that makes a first-time listener feel the assigned emotion. Spend
   real time here; fit matters more than any other music variable.

Never run one track for a whole video: constant tone is the seasoning problem
again — boring. Shifting beds keep the viewer alert and let contrast amplify
each section.

Ducking: voice stays king. Beds sit clearly under narration and dip further
under key lines; a bed that fights the voice costs comprehension, which costs
everything.

## 5. Music manipulation tricks (attention controls)

- **Abrupt pause:** stop the music dead for a special moment. The sudden
  change jolts attention and spotlights dialogue or a sound effect. Use for
  verdicts, confessions, and turns.
- **Slow fade-out:** signal a segment is ending. Once the viewer notices the
  music leaving, they anticipate something new — a micro re-hook built from
  absence.
- **Sync hits and dips to picture:** line music peaks, drops, and silences to
  the exact frame where the topic shifts (e.g., problem → solution). The sync
  marks the shift unmistakably and makes the new section feel more exciting.
  When stems are available, layer and automate them (drums in for tension,
  out for reflection) instead of swapping whole tracks.
- **Ride one track's internal arc first.** Before swapping beds at a boundary,
  check whether the current track already turns (somber to upbeat, sparse to
  dense): aligning its built-in shift to the picture turn carries the segment
  change with zero swap jarring. Only reach for a new track when no internal
  arc fits.

## Exit checklist

- [ ] Sound library gathered before the pass; no silent movements, entrances,
  or highlights remain.
- [ ] Risers only precede real payoffs; hits emphasize verdicts; drones only
  under genuine uncertainty.
- [ ] One bed per mood block, each chosen for the assigned emotion; voice
  clearly on top throughout.
- [ ] At least one manipulation moment placed (pause, fade, or synced
  hit/dip) at the video's most important shift.
