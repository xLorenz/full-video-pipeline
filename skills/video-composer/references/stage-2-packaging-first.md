# Stage 2 — Packaging-First Validation

> Prove the video can earn a click before spending a day making it. If you
> cannot build 3+ viable packaging angles from the idea, kill or rework the
> idea now — production cannot save an unpackagable premise.

## 1. The gate rule

Before any scripting, produce at least three distinct (thumbnail concept +
title angle) pairs for the premise. Each pair must contain:

1. One visual hook (object, situation, contrast, or symbol — Stage 8).
2. One title question or promise (not a summary — Stage 8).
3. One trust signal (proof, credential, specificity, or specificity of payoff).

If you cannot reach three pairs, the idea fails the gate. Options: find a more
uncertain angle, attach stakes, add a progression or challenge, or drop the
idea. Do not "produce first, package later" — the strongest creators treat
these tactics as idea criteria.

Why: packaging buys the viewer. The best script in the world retains zero
people if nobody clicks, and retrofitting a hook onto a finished video
produces vague, mismatched packaging that collapses in the first 30 seconds.

## 2. Angle-finding sprint (20 minutes)

1. List every interesting moment, fact, or visual the video could contain.
   Include the peak 5 seconds, the most counterintuitive finding, the biggest
   mistake revealed, the most visual transformation.
2. Circle the one with the widest curiosity (not your favorite — the broadest
   stranger's favorite). That is the lead angle; the rest become mid-video
   loops.
3. For the lead angle, draft:
   - The 1-second thumbnail read (what is seen + felt in one glance).
   - The title as the question the video answers (not what happens).
   - The immediate reason to watch in 5 words (a stranger must be able to
     state the payoff that fast).
4. Repeat for two alternative angles (different emotion: e.g., fear vs wonder
   vs ambition; or different hook type: mystery vs mistake vs spectacle).

Common mistake: packaging the whole video ("everything about X") instead of
selling the 1–2 most intriguing parts to the broadest audience. Hook broad,
expand inside.

## 3. Compatibility pre-check

Each surviving angle must pass:

- **Intro-payable in 30 seconds.** Can the first shot and first line show or
  restate the thumbnail situation + title question? If the angle requires
  3 minutes of context before it makes sense, it is not a packaging angle —
  pick a faster one. Mismatch causes mass early abandonment.
- **Payoff-honest.** Does the video actually deliver the promised secret,
  spectacle, or answer quickly and substantively? Promising a secret and
  delivering common knowledge, or promising spectacle and burying it, destroys
  trust and retention. Bold promises need proof anchors.
- **Visually stageable.** Can the thumbnail situation be composited or
  illustrated legibly at feed size (Stage 8: build from scratch, don't
  screenshot)? If the hook is purely abstract with no familiar visual anchor,
  invent the symbol now or choose a different angle.

## 4. Kill / rework triggers

Kill or fundamentally rework when:

- The title must explain the thumbnail and the thumbnail must explain the
  title (neither intrigues alone).
- The hook needs >1 second to decode (clutter, jargon, tiny objects,
  paragraphs).
- The missing piece feels unimportant (a puzzle about a low-stakes update
  with no danger, loss, or wonder attached).
- The surprise is well-known to the target audience (no expectation to
  violate).
- Three angles all sound like summaries ("How X shaped Y", "My process for
  Z") with no question, no stakes, no wonder.

Rework moves: attach a mistake frame ("you're doing X wrong"), attach a
hidden-cause frame ("the real reason X"), attach a clock or ranking, or
narrow to the single most visual sub-story.

## Exit checklist

- [ ] 3+ (thumbnail + title + trust signal) angles written down.
- [ ] Each angle states its payoff in 5 words a stranger could repeat.
- [ ] Lead angle is intro-payable in 30 seconds and payoff-honest.
- [ ] Visual hook is stageable at feed size (symbol/object/scene named).
- [ ] If gate failed: kill/rework decision recorded, not "we'll fix it later".
