# Appendix X — Shorts Adaptation (Swipe-Native Rules)

> Long-form rules (loops, clarity, packaging honesty) still apply, except
> exactly these four overrides: (1) Stage 4 zero-gap bridging OFF past the
> payoff — no mid-video bridges after resolution; (2) Stage 3 §9 Resolution
> OFF — no closure montage; (3) Stage 4 session bridge OFF — the catalog
> bridge becomes a rewatch loop only; (4) Stage 3 hook stack OFF — use the §1
> swipe hook, not the long-form priority list. Shorts have no click intent:
> the viewer did not choose your video, and a single boring sentence is a
> swipe. Read this file instead of Stages 3–4 defaults whenever the
> deliverable is under ~90 seconds.

## 1. Swipe-native opening (0–2 seconds)

There is no thumbnail and no title to do the work — judgment happens on
autoplay. The first sentence has exactly one job: deliver what the viewer
needs to know in the most direct words possible, while frame one shows
eye-catching visual proof simultaneously. No setup, no branding, no
"welcome back." If the opening does not give enough information in two
seconds to stay for the payoff, nothing else matters.

Swipe-native version of the two-tier hook (Stage 3 §4): frame one plus the
first sentence carry **recognition** — the specific problem this viewer has
right now, in words and images only they would recognize — and the next
sentence carries the **reason to listen** (own credibility, borrowed
credibility, or proof). Recognition matters *more* here than in long-form:
the swipe decision lands in 0–2 seconds, before curiosity has any time to
build, so the viewer is selecting on "is this about me," not "am I intrigued."

## 2. Compressed sentence skeleton (~30-second horizon)

Map the whole Short to sentences, each with one job:

1. **Hook** — direct payoff promise (sentence 1).
2. **Supporting hook or skip** — subvert, add context, or make the premise
   even bigger; but if the concept alone carries it, skip straight to scene.
   Never sell past the premise's strength.
3. **Set scene** — timeline or location plus the minimum context to follow;
   never over-explain what viewers can infer. Two sentences that raise
   curiosity beat five that exhaust it.
4. **Sell stakes** — restate the same question at higher stakes
   (1–2 sentences turning curiosity into investment).
5. **Edge, don't resolve** — tease with hints throughout; let the viewer feel
   they are solving a puzzle. Even correct early guessers stay to be 100%
   certain.
6. **Terminal payoff** — see §3.

Front-load concrete stakes early: a specific number or trigger word in the
first five seconds outperforms the same fact buried later.

**Extended skeleton (45–90 seconds).** Past roughly 45 seconds the middle can
no longer be one block — a single undifferentiated body has no interior exit
points and sags in the second half. Alternate Body → Re-hook:

- **Body n** — advances exactly one idea, told in a framing the audience has
  not already heard from competitors (Stage 3 §7).
- **Re-hook n** — renews curiosity *before* the next body opens, using the
  Stage 4 §4 devices (false binary, belief contradiction, escalation ladder,
  conditional gate). Pre-payoff bridging is fine; the override above only kills
  bridges *after* the payoff.

Below ~45 seconds, skip re-hooks entirely: there is no runway to open and close
a second loop before the terminal payoff, and a re-hook fired too close to the
payoff cuts it in half. The signal that you need them is simple — if you cannot
state the middle as one sentence, you have two bodies and need a re-hook
between them.

## 3. Terminal payoff discipline (overrides Stage 4 bridging)

Deliver the answer instantly at 75–85% runtime, then a tail of at most 5
seconds containing the loop-line and, optionally, the tied CTA fused into ONE
sentence. Nothing else past the payoff — no summary, no lingering, no second
bridge. Receipt equals exit; the tail exists only to loop or convert.

## 4. Rewatch loop + tied CTA tail

- **Loop the ending into the opening** where possible so replays feel
  seamless — rewatches are Shorts retention.
- **Exactly one CTA sentence**, post-payoff only, tied to the viewer's
  in-video action ("subscribe if you called it"). The tie converts investment
  to engagement; it is the sole allowed off-topic moment.

## Exit checklist (Shorts)

- [ ] First sentence = fastest direct delivery + frame-one visual proof.
- [ ] Recognition before intrigue: frame one plus sentence 1 name the
  viewer's concrete current problem; sentence 2 supplies the reason to listen.
- [ ] Skeleton mapped per §2; supporting hook skipped if premise carries.
- [ ] Over ~45s: alternating Body/Re-hook with one idea per body in an
  unfamiliar framing. Under ~45s: no re-hook, middle is one block.
- [ ] No over-explained simples; stakes restated higher before the middle.
- [ ] Payoff instant at 75–85%; tail ≤5s with loop-line + fused CTA at most.
- [ ] One tied CTA, nothing else off-topic; zero boring sentences.
