# Expressive Movements

Mid-century Modern · Psychedelic · Punk/New Wave · Memphis/Postmodern · Grunge

These styles trade neutrality for voice. Each has a *reason* for its excess — know it, or the work reads as costume.

---

## Mid-Century Modern (1940s–1960s) — American warmth

**Signals:** optimistic, human, playful, approachable, "premium but not cold".

**Defining characteristics.** European modernist geometry plus warmth, wit and narrative. Forms are bold and simplified but keep hand-crafted quality — visible paper-cut texture, woodblock grain, slightly irregular hand-drawn curves. Warm saturated palettes: mustard, burnt orange, olive, teal, charcoal. Illustration favored alongside or instead of photography, flat and abstracted to essential shapes. Type mixes sans headlines with more varied text faces, often custom-drawn per project. Paul Rand proved modernism could serve commerce at the highest level; Saul Bass made graphic design temporal (film titles unfolding over time).

**Build it:**
- Simplify the subject to 3–5 flat shapes max; add texture (grain, paper edge, halftone) rather than shading.
- Compose with witty asymmetry and confident negative space; let elements overlap slightly.
- Type: geometric or grotesque sans for headlines (often custom-tweaked), warm text face below; occasional script/brush accent for personality.
- Texture layer is mandatory: subtle noise/grain over flat color is what separates this from generic flat illustration.
- Avoid 3D shading, gradients, and modern UI cues.

**Palette seeds:** mustard `#E0A32E`, burnt orange `#C75B39`, olive `#6B7A45`, teal `#2A7F8E`, cream `#F3E9D2`, charcoal `#3A3A3A`, dusty pink `#D98E8E`.

**Today:** the most commercially referenced historical style — startup branding, coffee shops, co-working, lifestyle brands (Mailchimp 2018, Slack identity). Illustrators like Mads Berg and Owen Davey descend from it.

## Psychedelic (1960s–1970s) — expanding consciousness through form

**Signals:** altered states, counterculture, sensory overload, ecstatic energy.

**Defining characteristics.** Deliberate attack on legibility and visual comfort. Typography distorted, stretched, melted into organic forms pushed far beyond Art Nouveau — letterforms nearly unreadable, demanding active participation. Colors chosen for maximum optical vibration: red on blue-green, orange on purple, yellow on violet (complementary pairings creating shimmering instability). Dense, all-over, horror-vacui compositions. Imagery from Eastern mysticism, Surrealism, comics, Art Nouveau botanicals. Victor Moscoso deliberately exploited Albers' color-opposite effects rather than avoiding them.

**Build it:**
- Vibration first: choose complementary hue pairs at high saturation for adjacent forms; expect edges to shimmer.
- Liquid type: letters as shapes that flow, pulse, bulge, and interlock with imagery; hierarchy comes from scale and color, not weight.
- Fill the frame: border ornament, radiating motifs, all-over pattern; center-out energy or concentric mandala logic.
- Layers: flat color separations, overprint effects (multiply blending), no gradients.
- Legibility may be sacrificed — but only for *display* type, and only when the name is already known (concert posters of known bands). Never sacrifice the one thing the viewer must learn.

**Palette seeds:** hot red `#E4002B`, electric blue `#0047AB`, violet `#7D26CD`, acid yellow `#F4E04D`, orange `#FF6B1A`, green `#00A86B`, magenta `#D6006E`.

**Today:** cannabis branding, festival identities, craft beverage labels, music graphics. Its lesson — design can be experiential, not merely informational — still provokes.

## Punk & New Wave (1970s–1980s) — destroy and rebuild

**Signals:** authenticity, anti-establishment, DIY honesty, urgency.

**Defining characteristics.** Deliberate amateurism and confrontation. Hand-scrawled, photocopied, ransom-note type (cut from newspapers); typefaces aggressively misused, sizes violating every convention. Collaged imagery from found photography, altered with scribbles/defacement. Color reduced to black and white (photocopier economics) or harsh clashing combinations. Jamie Reid's Sex Pistols work declared that professional polish is complicit with the establishment. New Wave (Brody's *The Face*) kept the rule-breaking energy but applied real typographic literacy — deconstructed grids, layered type, experimental but editorially functional.

**Build it:**
- Ransom-note assembly: multiple typefaces/weights/cases at conflicting sizes, slightly rotated, overlapping, sometimes with visible paste-up edges.
- Photocopy aesthetics: high-contrast threshold images, halftone dots blown up, xerox grain, paper texture, tape, staples, marker overprint.
- Layout: anti-grid by default — elements collide, margins are violated, information density is high and slightly chaotic.
- But keep a legible core: event name, date, place must survive the chaos (the New Wave lesson).
- Black + one harsh spot color is the most authentic palette.

**Palette seeds:** xerox black `#0D0D0D`, paper white `#EDEDED`, safety red `#FF2A00`, fluoro pink `#FF3B8B`, or pure two-tone black/white.

**Today:** zine culture, protest graphics, independent music, and any context where polish would signal inauthenticity. The "undesigned" look of many DTC launches works in its shadow. New Wave's experiments seeded 1990s digital type.

## Memphis / Postmodernism (1980s) — serious play

**Signals:** wit, irreverence, anti-corporate joy, design-literate in-jokes.

**Defining characteristics.** Visual assault of clashing patterns, bright colors, and "bad taste" combinations. Squiggles, triangles, circles, squiggly lines in jarring arrangements mocking modernist seriousness. Bold repeating patterns (terrazzo, confetti, zigzags, squiggle grids) that carry no symbolism — decoration for decoration's sake, a direct challenge to "form follows function". Aggressively cheerful palette: hot pink, electric blue, acid yellow, bright red, mint. Type mixed anarchically across styles and weights. April Greiman embraced early Macintosh layering; Cranbrook (Katherine McCoy) engaged deconstruction and semiotics.

**Build it:**
- Pattern on pattern: combine 2–3 pattern types (dots + squiggles + terrazzo) in flat, saturated colors.
- Geometric confetti: scatter primitive shapes at varying sizes/rotations; some outlined, some filled.
- Type: mix classifications freely (serif + geometric sans + script); let them sit at odd angles or on patterned fields; contrast is the point.
- Composition: asymmetric, layered, overlapping planes; Memphis objects (stepped forms, "confetti" clusters) can act as illustrations.
- Keep *one* unifying thread (a repeated shape or a shared color) or the piece becomes chaos instead of play.

**Palette seeds:** hot pink `#FF4F81`, electric blue `#2E5BFF`, acid yellow `#F6E200`, bright red `#F63A2F`, mint `#6EE7B7`, lilac `#B28DFF`, cream base `#FFF8E7`, charcoal `#2B2B2B`.

**Today:** children's branding, tech office interiors, social graphics, "dopamine design" as antidote to minimalist blandness. Also the reference class for 1980s revival work.

## Grunge Typography & Design (1990s) — beautiful destruction

**Signals:** emotional rawness, authenticity over polish, cultural moment.

**Defining characteristics.** Distorted guitars translated to visual form: typography distressed, eroded, layered, sometimes actively illegible. Textures essential — photocopy artifacts, torn paper edges, ink bleeds, scratches, grain. Layouts reject grids for overlapping, colliding elements in controlled chaos. Dark muted palettes (browns, dark greens, burnt oranges, blacks) with occasional harsh contrast. Photography blurred, high-contrast, degraded. David Carson's *Ray Gun* (1992–95) subordinated readability to expression (an entire interview set in Zapf Dingbats). Emigre (VanderLans/Licko) gave typographic experiment a theoretical base.

**Build it:**
- Texture is the medium: layer grain, scratches, torn edges, halftone erosion, ink bleed over everything including type.
- Type: eroded/rough display faces, extreme weight contrast, text as texture (low-contrast, layered, partially obscured).
- Layout: collage logic — overlapping planes, elements crashing into each other, no clean columns; but establish a reading order underneath via scale and position.
- Color: dark, muddy, desaturated grounds; one or two high-contrast moments (a white word, a red slash).
- Degrade *deliberately*: rotate, scale non-uniformly, apply noise/erosion filters — but never degrade the one line of copy the viewer must read.

**Palette seeds:** espresso `#2B2118`, dark moss `#3E4A3D`, burnt sienna `#8A4B2B`, smoke gray `#6E6A66`, bone `#D6CFC2`, blood red `#7A1F1F`.

**Today:** neo-grunge/Y2K revivals, music and fashion graphics, editorial work wanting "raw". Its real legacy: emotional resonance is a legitimate design priority — every brand using rough texture or imperfect type works in grunge's shadow.
