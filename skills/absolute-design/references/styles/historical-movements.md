# Historical Movements

Arts & Crafts · Art Nouveau · Art Deco · Bauhaus · Swiss/International

Each entry: what it says · how to build it · palette seeds · type · modern use.

---

## Arts & Crafts (1880s–1910s) — the revolt against the machine

**Signals:** handmade care, artisanal quality, integrity, heritage, slow craft.

**Defining characteristics.** Dense, organic ornament drawn from nature — vines, birds, flowers, leaves in flat repeating patterns. Hand-lettered or medieval-manuscript-derived typography with heavy serifs and decorative initial capitals. Earthy, muted color. Deliberate absence of photographic/mechanical reproduction aesthetics. Every element looks placed by a human hand — because in the movement's ideal, it was. William Morris's Kelmscott Press treated page, text, illustration, border and binding as one unified object.

**Build it:**
- Ornament first: border frames, vine/leaf motifs, repeating natural patterns; flat shapes, no gradients, no shadows.
- Type: heavy old-style/blackletter-influenced serifs; drop caps; centered or fully justified text blocks with tight margins (Morris-style density).
- Layout: closed compositions, framed, symmetric, dense — whitespace is filled with pattern, not left open.
- Imagery: woodcut/linocut feel, flat color separation, visible texture.

**Palette seeds:** deep red `#7B2D26`, forest green `#2E4B3C`, indigo `#2C3A5A`, gold `#B8860B`, parchment `#F2E8D5`, ink brown `#3B2F2F`.

**Today:** craft branding, artisanal food & packaging, book arts, "maker movement" aesthetics, anything claiming hand-made value. Jessica Hische's lettering carries its DNA.

## Art Nouveau (1890s–1910s) — the organic line

**Signals:** elegance, sensuality, ornament as identity, fin-de-siècle luxury.

**Defining characteristics.** Sinuous whiplash curves — plant stems, petals, insect wings, the idealized human figure — used as the primary decorative element rather than dense interlocking pattern. Letterforms swell and taper like growing vines; typography and illustration fuse into one decorative surface. Compositions are open, with line doing the work. Mucha's signature: a central figure inside an elaborate decorative halo of organic forms and geometric (often Byzantine-inspired) borders.

**Build it:**
- Line quality is everything: long continuous curves, asymmetrical whiplash stems, tendrils that frame and connect elements.
- Integrate type into the ornament — arched text, lettering that follows the curve, custom swashes.
- Central figure / motif with a radiating decorative field around it.
- Flat-ish color with luminous pastels, or bold poster palettes; hairline detail within color fields.

**Palette seeds:** pale gold `#C9A227`, soft green `#8FBC8F`, lavender `#B39DDB`, rose `#E8A0A0`, cream `#FAF3E0`, deep plum `#4A2545` (for contrast).

**Today:** cosmetics and wine packaging, tattoo design, luxury branding, editorial illustration, any context wanting organic elegance. Revived by 1960s psychedelia (which pushed the curves to overload).

## Art Deco (1920s–1940s) — geometry meets glamour

**Signals:** luxury, celebration, metropolitan sophistication, machine-age confidence.

**Defining characteristics.** Where Nouveau curves, Deco angles: chevrons, sunbursts, zigzags, stepped profiles (ziggurats), radiating fans, parallel line groupings — all with luxurious precision. Strong vertical emphasis; symmetrical or near-symmetrical composition; extravagant inline/outline type treatments. Embraces the machine age: speed, industry, modernity, streamlined forms. A.M. Cassandre synthesized Cubist fragmentation with commercial clarity — avant-garde structure that reads instantly at poster scale.

**Build it:**
- Composition: centered, symmetric stacks; strong vertical axis; stepped/terraced forms narrowing upward.
- Motifs: sunburst rays from a focal point, fan arcs, chevron borders, parallel rules, zigzag steps, geometric florals reduced to angles.
- Type: geometric sans or geometric serif, tall and condensed; inline/outline/extruded effects; wide-tracked caps; often stacked in blocks.
- Imagery: stylized, streamlined, poster-flat — no soft gradients (metallic sheen gradients are acceptable and even characteristic).

**Palette seeds:** gold `#D4AF37`, black `#141414`, ivory `#F7F0DC`, teal `#0F6B6B`, coral `#E2725B`, silver `#B8B8B8`, deep navy `#0E1A38`.

**Today:** event invitations, cocktail bar and hospitality branding, high-end real estate, film-title energy. Its geometric clarity adapts beautifully to variable fonts and motion.

## Bauhaus (1919–1933) — form follows function

**Signals:** rationality, systematic thinking, industrial modernity, art-meets-technology.

**Defining characteristics.** Decoration stripped to structural bones. Compositions built from geometric primitives — circle, square, triangle — arranged asymmetrically but rigorously balanced. Primary colors + black + white. Sans-serif typography, often experimental (Bayer's 1925 "universal" alphabet reduced letters to geometric components). Photography integrated via collage/photomontage (Constructivist influence). Every element must serve a communicative function; ornament without function is rejected. Joost Schmidt: "Everything should start with a square or a circle."

**Build it:**
- Build the composition from primitives: place circles/squares/triangles/lines first as the compositional skeleton, then type within it.
- Asymmetric balance with clear tension axes; diagonal rules and bars allowed.
- Color blocking: one primary dominates, secondaries as small accents; large fields of flat color.
- Type: geometric sans, often all-lowercase (Bayer's anti-hierarchical impulse), set flush to geometric edges; type as a structural element, not a caption.
- Photomontage: hard-edged photo crops layered with flat shapes, halftone texture acceptable.

**Palette seeds:** red `#E63329`, yellow `#F0C808`, blue `#1F5FA9`, black `#111111`, white `#FAFAFA`, gray `#8A8A8A`.

**Today:** underlies virtually all modern design education and design systems (Material Design is Bauhaus logic applied to interfaces). Branding, packaging, environmental graphics, exhibition design.

## Swiss / International Typographic Style (1950s–1970s) — the grid takes over

**Signals:** institutional authority, objectivity, neutrality, precision, trust.

**Defining characteristics.** Mathematical grid construction; sans-serif typography (Helvetica, Univers, Akzidenz-Grotesk); flush-left/ragged-right text; objective photography over illustration; white space as a *structural* element rather than empty background; color used purposefully and sparingly. The overall effect: clarity, neutrality, institutional authority. Information organized on a visible or implied grid — **the grid is the design**. Müller-Brockmann's concert posters used pure geometric abstraction (arcs, circles, lines) to visualize rhythm; Armin Hofmann emphasized contrast and figure–ground in black-and-white.

**Build it:**
- Construct a real column grid first (5–12 columns). Place *everything* on it; flush-left/ragged-right only.
- Type: one neogrotesque sans, few weights, strong size contrast between levels; generous letter-spacing on small caps labels; body at a comfortable measure.
- Whitespace is composed: margins and gaps vary by hierarchy (tight within groups, generous between).
- Imagery: objective photography, hard rectangular crops aligned to the grid; or pure geometric abstraction (arcs, circles, rules).
- Color: near-monochrome (black on white) + one accent used with discipline; or a single structural color field.
- Break nothing unless the break is the concept.

**Palette seeds:** paper white `#F7F7F5`, ink black `#1A1A1A`, signal red `#E1251B` (classic Swiss accent), mid gray `#9B9B9B`, cool gray ramp, occasional deep blue `#1B3A6B`.

**Today:** the invisible default of professional design — corporate identity, wayfinding, government and scientific communication, editorial systems, and every design system framework. When people say "clean design," they usually mean Swiss.
