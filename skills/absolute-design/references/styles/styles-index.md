# Styles — diagnose, choose, mix

A style is a dialect with a history. Using it well means knowing what it *says* — Swiss is not "clean", it is institutional authority earned by the mid-century Swiss design establishment; grunge is not "messy", it is an argument that emotional resonance outranks clean communication. Historical literacy is what stops a design from accidentally plagiarizing a movement and calling it an idea.

---

## 1. Diagnose a style (or a request) in 5 questions

Ask these of any visual reference, or of the brief:

1. **Line quality** — organic curves (Art Nouveau, psychedelic) or geometric angles (Art Deco, Bauhaus, Swiss)?
2. **Space density** — densely filled / horror vacui (Arts & Crafts, psychedelic, maximalism) or open and restrained (Swiss, minimalism)?
3. **Type–image relationship** — integrated/decorative (Art Nouveau, psychedelic) or clearly separated with type in charge (Swiss, minimalism)?
4. **Finish** — polished (Swiss, Art Deco, flat) or raw (grunge, punk, brutalism)?
5. **Historical stance** — sincere revival (mid-century) or ironic/deconstructed reference (postmodern/Memphis)?

These five answers place most work within one or two traditions.

## 2. Choose by what the work must signal

| The brief wants… | Style family | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Authority, clarity, institutional trust | Swiss / International | the professional default of "clean"; institutional neutrality |
| Heritage, craft, artisanal value | Arts & Crafts, Art Nouveau | hand-work visible; ornament as care |
| Luxury, celebration, metropolitan glamour | Art Deco | geometry + metallics = Jazz Age sophistication |
| Systematic modernity, product rigor | Bauhaus, Flat | primitives + function-first logic |
| Warmth, approachable, nostalgic-but-current | Mid-century | modernist structure + human warmth |
| Rebellion, authenticity, subculture | Punk, Grunge, Brutalism | rawness reads as honesty; polish reads as complicity |
| Play, joy, anti-corporate energy | Memphis, Maximalism | abundance and clash as an antidote to blandness |
| Premium restraint, editorial calm | Minimalism | whitespace as status |
| Attention at all costs (feed, poster, event) | Psychedelic, Maximalism, Brutalist type | visual vibration and volume |

**The pendulum matters:** culture alternates restraint and excess (Swiss → postmodernism → digital minimalism → maximalism/blunt brutalism). If every competitor is minimalist, a maximalist or brutalist approach differentiates — and vice versa. Style choice is market positioning.

## 3. Mixing styles — the dominant-framework rule

Contemporary work samples and remixes constantly, and it works when it is hierarchical:

1. **Choose one dominant style** — it owns the grid, the primary typography, and the overall tone.
2. **Introduce a second style only as accent** — its color logic, its ornament, or one typographic device, never its whole system.
3. **Keep the accent count low** (one device per piece ideally). Incoherence comes from competing systems, not from variety itself.

Proven hybrids: Swiss grid + Memphis color and pattern; Bauhaus typography + maximalist composition; Swiss clarity + punk rawness (Sagmeister-style hand lettering on photographic surfaces); Swiss grid logic + expressionist scale (Paula Scher's environmental graphics).

The danger sign: two styles both claiming the type. If the display type is Art Deco *and* the body is Swiss, that's hierarchy (fine). If they clash at the same size, it's noise.

## 4. Style fidelity check (run before delivery)

For the chosen style, verify all of:

- [ ] **Typography** matches the movement's voice (see each style file) — the most common tell of a fake style is generic type in period costume
- [ ] **Color** matches the movement's palette logic (metallics for Deco, primaries for Bauhaus, clashing accents for Memphis…)
- [ ] **Ornament** matches the movement's rules (absent for Swiss/minimalism; structural for Deco; organic for Nouveau; pattern-on-pattern for Memphis)
- [ ] **Composition logic** matches (symmetry for Deco ceremony; asymmetric grid for Swiss; fractured/tilted for postmodern)
- [ ] **No anachronistic tells** (drop shadows and glassy gradients in a Swiss poster; flat design in an Art Nouveau piece — unless deliberate remix, stated as such)
